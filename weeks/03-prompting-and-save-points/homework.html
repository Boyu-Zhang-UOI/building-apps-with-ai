Week 3 Homework ​

Due before next week's Studio A (default 11:59 pm the night before). Core: about 5 hours plus the journal, required. Stretch: optional. Graded on effort and completion of the core tier (rubric). "On Canvas" means the course's learning management system, not Gemini's Canvas tool.

Use the Safe Loop for every change: Describe → Plan → Step → Test → Read → Commit.

Deliverables checklist ​

  • ☐ My game works at https://[your-username].github.io/game/ through every step of my plan (or I've recorded the blocker in PROMPTS.md), with one commit per working step; each AI-helped commit ends with an Assisted-by: line (task 1).
  • ☐ I fixed the first thing my play-tester found and at least one edge case, and my home page links to the game by name (task 2).
  • ☐ PROMPTS.md has my three diff readings (🔴 AI off) and my giant-prompt experiment (tasks 3 and 4).
  • ☐ PROMPTS.md has this week's entries in four-part form (task 6).
  • ☐ On Canvas: my weekly reflection (🔴 AI off), including the paragraph on the reading (tasks 5 and 7).

Core ​

1. Finish your game (2 h) ​

🟢 AI on.

Pick up where the timer stopped. For each remaining step: one step prompt with your current file pasted in, test it against its "Done when" and one edge case, read the diff, and commit with an Assisted-by: line. Two failed fixes? Use the two-strikes rule.

✅ Checkpoint: https://[your-username].github.io/game/ works through step 5, and the file's History has one clear commit per working step.

2. Play-test, edge cases and the link (1 h) ​

🟢 AI on for the fixes · 🔴 AI off for the link text.

  1. Fix the first thing that confused your play-tester in Studio B, with a step prompt (Goal: fix [problem]; Context: the complete file; Constraints: change only that; Done when: the problem no longer happens and everything else still works). Test, read the diff, and commit as Fix: [what you fixed]. No play-tester? Ask someone at home to play it for two minutes without help.
  2. Test three edge cases and fix at least one that breaks. Ideas: restart twice in a row; press keys that aren't controls; make the window very narrow; click very fast; lose on purpose in the first second.
  3. By hand (🔴 AI off), in github.dev, open your home page's index.html and find the Projects link to game/. Change its text from the placeholder to your game's name, for example Snake, built in one hour. Commit: Link game from home page (no Assisted-by: line: you did it yourself).

✅ Checkpoint: clicking the Projects link on your live home page opens your game, and its History has at least one Fix: commit.

3. Read three diffs (30 min) ​

🔴 AI off until the last step.

Why: Simon Willison's golden rule (task 5) is to commit only code you could explain to someone else. Reading your own diffs is practice for that, and for next week's peer explanation swap.

  1. On github.com, open game/index.html → History, and pick three of your step commits.
  2. For each one, click it and read the red and green lines. In PROMPTS.md, under a heading Diffs I read, write two lines:
    • What changed in the code, in your own words (for example: "added a score variable and a line in eatFood() that adds 1 to it").
    • Does the commit message say so? If not, write the message you'd use now.
  3. If there's a change you can't explain, mark it ?. Only then ask your assistant to explain that part of the diff, and add one sentence on what you learned.

This is a first taste of the teach-back: from next week you write two or three sentences like these before each AI-helped commit.

4. The giant-prompt experiment (1 h) ​

🟢 AI on.

Why: the study in the Key ideas found that one giant prompt does best on the first try and worst when the code has to change. Test that on your own game.

  1. In a fresh chat, ask for your whole game in one prompt: paste the five steps of your plan and ask for the finished game in one file.
  2. Test it against your five "Done when" lines. Write down how many pass.
  3. In the same chat, ask for one change your play-tester wanted (or the fix from task 2). Test again: did anything that worked before break?
  4. Compare it with the version you built in steps. Under a heading Giant-prompt experiment in PROMPTS.md, write 4–6 sentences: how many checks passed first time, what happened when you asked for a change, which version you could explain more of, and which one you'd rather keep changing.

Don't commit the giant version into game/index.html. If you want to keep it, save it as game/giant.html and commit it with an Assisted-by: line.

5. Reading (30 min) ​

Read Simon Willison's "Not all AI-assisted programming is vibe coding (but vibe coding rocks)" (about 20 minutes). It's the post behind the course's golden rule. Then add a short paragraph to your reflection (task 7): in your own words, what is his golden rule, and describe one moment this week when you followed it or broke it.

6. PROMPTS.md (15 min) ​

🟢 AI on for formatting · 🔴 AI off for "What I wrote or decided myself".

Add a Week 3 heading, with the prompts written in four-part form (Goal · Context · Constraints · Done when):

  • your Studio A practice prompt, and the link to its Assisted-by: commit;
  • your plan prompt, and how you changed the plan;
  • at least three step prompts, including one that failed and what you did next;
  • the play-test: what confused your tester, and your fix;
  • your diff readings (task 3) and the giant-prompt experiment (task 4).

Update What I wrote or decided myself (the link text, and any change you made by hand) and What I'd prompt differently next time. Commit with a clear message.

7. Weekly reflection (20 min) ​

🔴 AI off.

Answer the four questions in the reflection template in 150–300 words, plus the reading paragraph from task 5, without AI. Good moments to write about: the buzzer, a step that took two strikes, or what the giant-prompt experiment showed you. Submit it on Canvas, not in your repository (Privacy of your work).

Stretch (optional) ​

Each of these is a small Safe Loop of its own. Commit before you start and after each working step, with Assisted-by: lines, and log your prompts in PROMPTS.md.

  • A ★★★ Hard game from the game menu, as a second game in game2/index.html, linked from your Projects section.
  • Read the study behind "small steps": Kazemitabaar et al. (Koli Calling 2023). Read the abstract and the conclusion. Does it match what your giant-prompt experiment showed?

IMPORTANT

Minimum to keep up: your game is at /game/ with at least three working-step commits. Next week you break it on purpose and restore it from those save points, compare two AI models on its next change, and write your first automated test for one of its functions. If it isn't working, ask your instructor for help before Studio A.
