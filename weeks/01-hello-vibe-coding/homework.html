Week 1 Homework ​

Due before next week's Studio A (default 11:59 pm the night before). Core: about 5 hours including project time, required. Stretch: optional. Graded on effort and completion of the core tier (rubric). "On Canvas" means the course's learning management system, not Gemini's Canvas tool.

Deliverables checklist ​

  • ☐ My index.html has at least three sections, at least five customization prompts behind it, and opens from my computer (task 1).
  • ☐ It includes one small feature I wrote entirely by hand (🔴 AI off), and the latest version is uploaded to Canvas as "Week 1 page" (task 2).
  • ☐ My scratch note lists the prompts that mattered and every change I made by hand (tasks 1–2). Next week it becomes PROMPTS.md.
  • ☐ I worked through the first half of the AI Fluency course (task 3). The response is due in week 2.
  • ☐ On Canvas: the signed safety contract and my pre-course self-assessment; my GitHub Education application is sent (task 4).
  • ☐ On Canvas: my weekly reflection (🔴 AI off) (task 5).

Core ​

1. Finish your page (1 h 30 min) ​

🟢 AI on.

  1. Start from your current file. Open a new chat and paste your saved index.html first, with a line such as "Here is my current page. I changed some parts by hand; keep them." The AI can't see your hand changes otherwise.
  2. Reach at least five customization prompts in total, counting the ones from Studio A. One change per prompt; predict before you look; check against your "Done when". Ideas: customization prompts.
  3. Have at least three sections, for example "About me", "Things I'm learning" and one of your choice (nothing personal). Next week you add a fourth, "Projects".
  4. Check it at phone size: drag your browser window narrow. Nothing should stick out sideways.
  5. Save as you go. After each change that works, save index.html. Before a risky change, keep a copy of the last good version (for example index-good.html). That's a save point by hand; in week 3, git does this for you.
  6. Log each prompt that mattered in your scratch note: the prompt, what happened, what you checked.

✅ Checkpoint: index.html opens from your computer with at least three sections, and your scratch note lists at least five customization prompts.

2. Write one feature by hand (30 min) ​

🔴 AI off. You may use your own file, the bridge page and MDN.

Add a second button that does something different from the first, written entirely by you. Pick one:

  • it switches the page between two color themes;
  • it shows or hides one of your sections;
  • it counts how many times it has been clicked, and shows the number.

You write all three parts: the <button> in the HTML, at least one CSS rule for it, and the JavaScript that finds it and listens for a click. Your existing fun-fact button is a working model: read how it does each part, then write your own. Predict, save, refresh, check.

Stuck after 20 minutes? Write down exactly where (which part, what you expected, what happened) in your scratch note and stop: that counts. If you need a hint, the JavaScript has this shape:

js
const myButton = document.getElementById("[your button's id]");
myButton.addEventListener("click", () => {
  // change something on the page here
});

Then upload the latest index.html to Canvas as "Week 1 page" (it replaces the Studio B upload), and add the feature to your scratch note's list of hand changes.

✅ Checkpoint: clicking your new button does what you planned, the fun-fact button still works, and Canvas has the latest file.

3. AI fluency pre-work, part 1 (1 h 30 min) ​

🟢 AI on for the course's own exercises.

What: Anthropic's free online course AI Fluency: Framework and foundations, or an equivalent your instructor names. It is about working well with any AI tool, not only Anthropic's. It names four skills: Delegation (deciding what to hand to an AI), Description (telling it clearly what you want), Discernment (judging what comes back) and Diligence (taking responsibility for what you do with it). It's also in the Reading List.

  1. Open the course and click Start course. You don't need an account to start; signing in only saves your progress, and you don't need a Claude account at all.
  2. This week, work from the start through the lessons on Delegation (about 1½ hours). If the lesson list has changed, stop at about the halfway point. Week 2 covers the rest and a short response.
  3. As you go, jot down one moment from building your page that fits a lesson. You'll use these notes in next week's response.

4. Week 0 tasks for week 1 (40 min) ​

These are in the "During week 1" section of the week 0 pre-work; the details are there.

  • Sign the safety contract on Canvas (15 min). Read each rule and its plain-English summary. You sign on Canvas, not in a repository.
  • Take the pre-course self-assessment on Canvas (15 min, 🔴 AI off). There are no wrong answers. You'll take it again at the end of the course to see how far you came.
  • Apply for GitHub Education (10 min). Approval can take a few days, so apply now.

5. Weekly reflection (30 min) ​

🔴 AI off.

Answer questions 1–4 of the reflection template in 150–300 words, in your own words, without AI. Name specific moments: a prompt, a surprise in the preview, a line you changed. This week, also answer: which part of your page did the AI's tour explain well, and which part did you only understand once you changed it yourself? Submit it on Canvas, not in a repository: reflections are personal writing (Privacy of your work).

Stretch (optional) ​

Same prompt, second assistant ​

Send your first prompt from the lab, unchanged, to a second assistant from the Tool Handout. Compare the two results in your scratch note:

QuestionAssistant 1Assistant 2
Did it meet every "Done when" item?
What did it add that you didn't ask for?
Roughly how many lines of code?
Which would you rather change by hand? Why?

A fun-fact button that never repeats ​

🔴 AI off. Change the fun-fact button so it never shows the same fact twice in a row. You'll need to remember the last fact shown and pick again while the new one matches it. Test it by clicking twenty times.

IMPORTANT

Minimum to keep up: your index.html opens in your browser and contains at least one change you made by hand. Week 2's Studio A publishes it, so bring it on your laptop (or download it from Canvas). If you have no working file, ask your instructor for the starter page before Studio A.
