Week 5 Lab — Spec First ​

AI-use key: 🟢 AI on: use AI freely · 🟡 AI limited: the AI may comment or hint, but the work is yours · 🔴 AI off: close the AI tabs and work alone.

This week you write the spec for Project 1: A Tool I'd Actually Use. In Studio A you size your idea and write SPEC.md by hand. In Studio B a classmate reviews it, a fresh AI that has never heard of you says what it can't understand (the Goldfish test), and the spec moves into its own repository. Next week an app builder builds from it, and then you do.

You need: your paper sketch (or paper and a pen), a laptop, your GitHub account, a chat assistant, and Canvas, the course's learning management system.

WARNING

Nothing in your spec should be personal data: not yours, and not anyone else's. In Studio B you paste it into an AI, and next week into app builders. On some free tiers, including Google AI Studio's, human reviewers may read what you type (Tool Handout). Your spec describes a tool, not a person.

Using a screen reader or only a keyboard? See the screen reader and keyboard guide. Working alone? Each pair activity has a Working alone? line.

Part 1 — Size your Project 1 idea (5 min) ​

Why: a tool you would really use gives you a real user to design for (you) and a reason to finish.

Project 1 has four fixed rules:

  1. Single user. Only you use it. No sharing, no other people's accounts.
  2. Data saved in the browser with localStorage (the browser's small built-in storage for one website). Nothing goes to a server.
  3. No login.
  4. No real personal data about other people. Use made-up names if you need any.

Read this list before class. Pick one idea from it, or bring your own that follows the rules.

IdeaWhat it doesGood edge cases to think about
Habit trackerTick off daily habits; show a streakMissing a day; a new day starting at midnight
Plant watering trackerList plants and how often they need water; show "water today"A plant added today; changing the schedule
Workout logRecord exercises, sets and reps; show personal bestsA zero or negative weight; two bests on one day
FlashcardsMake cards for a subject; sort into "know" and "still learning"An empty deck; a very long answer
Recipe box and weekly meal planSave recipes; pick them into daysA recipe used twice; deleting a recipe that's planned
Packing listReusable lists for trips; tick items offResetting ticks for the next trip; duplicate items
Watch listFilms and series to watch; mark watched; rateDuplicate titles; rating out of range
Vocabulary builderWords and translations; a quick self-quizAccents and special characters; an empty list
Spending diaryYour own spending (or made-up numbers) by category, per monthDecimals like 0.10 + 0.20; a new month
Mood or energy journalRate 1–5 with a short note, just for youTwo entries on one day; a very long note

TIP

Size check. A good Project 1 has one main screen and 3–5 MUST stories, and you could explain the whole idea in one breath. Save these for later projects: logins and sharing (weeks 11–14), anything that calls the internet or an AI (weeks 9–10), payments (never), photo uploads (the browser's storage is too small).

Tell your neighbor your idea in one breath. If it takes two, cut something.

✅ Checkpoint: you can finish this sentence out loud: "It's a tool that helps me [do what] so that [why it matters]."

Part 2 — Write SPEC.md yourself (35 min) ​

🔴 AI off. The spec is your thinking. The AI gets to question it in Studio B, but not to write it. Close every AI tab, and switch off any writing assistant built into your notes app or document editor.

Why: everyone and everything that reads your spec next can only build what it says.

  1. Open the SPEC template and copy all of its text. On GitHub, use the Copy raw file button at the top right of the file; on the course website, use the template's copy button.
  2. Paste it somewhere you can keep it until Studio B: a notes app, a document or a text editor. (It goes into your repository in Part 5.)
  3. Fill in the sections in this order. Use the times as a guide.
SectionTimeTips
1. Problem and 2. User5 minBe specific: when, where, how often, what annoys you now. The user can be you
3. User stories7 minAs a …, I want to …, so that … Mark each MUST (useless without it), SHOULD (want it soon) or COULD (nice later). Aim for 3–5 MUSTs
4. Acceptance criteria12 minAt least one per MUST story, plus at least two edge cases. How to write them is below
5. Out of scope and 6. Constraints5 minName what an AI is likely to add on its own: login, sync, charts, dark mode. Keep the Project 1 constraints: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript (index.html, style.css, app.js, with the rules in their own module so they can be tested), localStorage, no frameworks, no build step
7. Wireframe3 minDescribe your paper sketch from top to bottom in a short list (for example "title; input box with Add button; the list, newest first"). A list is easier for screen readers and for AIs than a text drawing
8. Open questions3 minAnything you haven't decided. Write your current best guess next to each one

How to write an acceptance criterion ​

An acceptance criterion says exactly what the app must do in one situation, so anyone can check it by using the app. This course writes them as:

WHEN a situation or action, THE APP SHALL an observable result.

Untestable (a vibe)Testable (a criterion)
The app should be easy to use.WHEN I open the app for the first time, THE APP SHALL show "No plants yet. Add your first plant above."
Watering works properly.WHEN I press Watered on a plant, THE APP SHALL move its next watering date forward by its schedule (for example, 3 days) and remove it from the "Water today" list.
It saves stuff.WHEN I reload the page, THE APP SHALL show the same plants and dates as before.
It handles bad input.WHEN I press Add with an empty name, THE APP SHALL add nothing and SHALL show "Give your plant a name."

Check every criterion against these four questions:

  • ☐ Could a stranger check it in under a minute, by using the app, without asking me anything?
  • ☐ Does it say what they would see (exact text in quotes where it matters)?
  • ☐ Does it describe one behavior? (Split "and also" into two criteria.)
  • ☐ Across the whole table, have I covered at least two edge cases? Try: empty input · very long input · duplicates · reloading the page · a small phone screen · zero or negative numbers · midnight or a new week · deleting the last item.

TIP

If you already program: say what, not how. Leave functions, classes and data structures out of the spec; a criterion describes what the user sees. Do use your instinct for boundaries: "WHEN I type a name of exactly 40 characters, THE APP SHALL accept it; WHEN I type 41, THE APP SHALL refuse it" is a sharp pair of criteria, and next week each becomes a one-line automated test.

Compare your draft with the example spec and its "Why this spec works" table.

✅ Checkpoint: every section is filled in, with 3–5 MUST stories, at least 5 acceptance criteria (at least two of them edge cases), and at least 3 out-of-scope items.

NOTE

Finding this hard is normal. In CMU's AI coding course, students rated writing the spec as harder than managing AI agents (CMU 15-113). It gets easier with every project.

Checkpoint share (5 min) ​

Two or three volunteers read out one criterion. The room applies the stranger test: could you check it in a minute without asking? If not, say what's missing.

Before you leave Studio A:

  • ☐ Your SPEC.md draft is saved somewhere you can open in Studio B.
  • ☐ Not finished? Finish it before Studio B, still 🔴 AI off. Studio B starts with the review.

Troubleshooting ​

ProblemTry this
I can't choose between two ideasChoose the one you'd use this week. Then apply the size check: one screen, 3–5 MUSTs
My idea needs a login, sharing or live dataCut it to the single-user core and put the rest in Out of scope. The big version may make a good capstone (capstone ideas)
My criteria say "fast", "nice" or "properly"Ask the stranger question: what would they see? Write that, in quotes
I can't think of edge casesGo down the edge-case list above, or ask: "What's the first thing a careless user would do?"
My spec is running onto a second pageToo big. Move SHOULD and COULD criteria to later and keep the MUSTs
The pasted template lost its tablesCopy the raw file (the Copy raw file button), not the rendered page

Part 3 — Peer spec review (25 min) ​

🔴 AI off. This review is graded (peer review rubric): it's specific, accurate and kind.

Why: a classmate reads your spec the way a builder will, without the picture in your head.

  1. Swap (5 min). Swap laptops with your partner, or share a view-only link. Read their spec silently.
  2. Talk (10 min, 5 per spec). The reviewer does three things while the author stays silent and notes every surprise:
    • Predict: without looking at the wireframe, describe the app you would build from this spec.
    • Find the untestable: point at any criterion (by ID) you couldn't check by using the app, and say why.
    • Find the missing edge: name one situation the spec doesn't cover.
  3. Write it up on Canvas (10 min). Open the Week 5 spec review assignment and write your review of your partner's spec under five headings: your prediction; every untestable criterion (by ID) and why; one missing edge case; one strength; one question for the author. Refer to your partner by GitHub username. Share it with them privately (a Canvas comment), never as a file, issue or comment in their repository. Not finished? Finish it by the homework deadline.

Authors: fix what you agree with now, or add it to "Open questions". Note what changed for your PROMPTS.md (write "my review partner", not a name).

✅ Checkpoint: you've given a review and received one, and your spec has changed in at least one place.

Working alone? Ask your instructor for the practice spec; your written review of it is the graded one. Then read your own spec aloud as if you were a stranger, or ask a friend to predict the app from it.

Part 4 — The Goldfish test (20 min) ​

🟡 AI limited. The AI reads your spec and asks questions. It doesn't rewrite your spec, and you don't paste back anything it writes.

Why: a fresh chat knows nothing about you, your idea or this class, like a goldfish that remembers nothing. Every app builder, chat assistant and agent that reads your spec starts that way. If a fresh chat can't understand your spec without asking you, neither can they.

  1. Open a brand-new chat in your chat assistant (Gemini, or a fallback from the Tool Handout): no earlier messages, and no explanation of your idea. If your assistant remembers past conversations, use a temporary chat.

  2. Paste this prompt, then your spec, and nothing else:

    text
    Goal: Tell me what is unclear in the spec below. Do NOT rewrite it and do NOT write any code.
Context: The spec below is all you know. Later you will have to build the app from it, with no chance to ask me anything.
Constraints: Reply with three short lists. "What I would build": the app in 5 bullet points, as you understand it. "Questions I'd have to ask": up to 6 questions the spec doesn't answer. "Guesses I'd make": up to 4 assumptions you would have to make, each with the criterion ID or section it affects. Don't suggest new features and don't write criteria for me.
Done when: I can compare your picture of the app with mine, and every question points to a part of my spec.

[paste your whole SPEC.md here]

  3. Compare (4 min). Read "What I would build". Mark every place it differs from the app in your head. Each question and each guess is a gap in your spec.

  4. Log it (4 min). In your notes, write this entry. It becomes your first PROMPTS.md entry in Part 5, and the Project 1 rubric looks for it.

    markdown
    ### [date] — Goldfish test of SPEC.md
- **Tool:** [chat assistant], fresh chat, spec only
- **What it asked or got wrong:** [for example: "Does the week start on Monday?" (AC7)]
- **What I changed in SPEC.md:** [for example: AC7 now says Monday to Sunday]

  5. Revise (8 min). Answer each question in the spec, in your own words, not in the chat. A feature you decide not to build goes into Out of scope.

IMPORTANT

If the AI rewrites your spec anyway, don't use its version. Reply "Please only list questions; don't rewrite." The course's AI policy lets an AI critique your spec, not write it.

✅ Checkpoint: your Goldfish log names at least two questions or wrong guesses, and your spec has changed in at least two places since Studio A (from the peer review and the Goldfish test).

Part 5 — Give your spec a home (15 min) ​

Why: from now on your spec lives in the same repository as your code, so every tool you use can read it and your changes have a history.

  1. Create the repository. On GitHub, click + → New repository. Name it after your tool, in lowercase with hyphens (for example plant-tracker). Choose Public and tick Add a README file. Click Create repository.
  2. Add your spec. Click Add file → Create new file. Name it SPEC.md, paste your spec, and commit with the message Add spec.
  3. Start your prompt log. Copy the PROMPTS template the same way you copied the SPEC template, and create a file called PROMPTS.md. Paste in your Goldfish log from Part 4, and one line saying what changed after the peer review. Commit with the message Start prompt log.
  4. Turn on GitHub Pages now, so it's ready next week. Go to Settings → Pages. Under "Build and deployment", choose Deploy from a branch, then branch main and folder / (root), and click Save. Menus move. If you can't find it, ask your chat assistant where the GitHub Pages setting is.

Your repository is public, so it holds only your project work: no classmates' names, no grades, no reflections. Those go on Canvas.

✅ Checkpoint: your repository shows README.md, SPEC.md and PROMPTS.md, and Settings → Pages shows your site address (it shows your README until you add index.html next week).

Part 6 — Polish the spec (10 min) ​

If time. Pick one:

  • Add your wireframe image. Take a screenshot of your sketch photo (a screenshot carries no location data) and save it as wireframe.png. On your repository page, click Add file → Upload files, drop it in, and commit. Then edit SPEC.md and add ![Wireframe: (one-line description)](wireframe.png) to section 7, keeping your top-to-bottom list.
  • Goldfish, round 2. Run Part 4 again in another brand-new chat with your revised spec. Does it ask fewer questions? Log the count. (This is homework anyway.)
  • Start your logic plan. Read homework task 4 and mark which of your criteria hide a rule: a check on input, a calculation, a date.

Exit ticket (10 min) ​

The week 5 exit ticket: 🔴 AI off, graded on completion, not correctness. The first question looks back at your week 3 game; answer it from memory, without opening your files.

ProblemTry this

The fresh chat already seems to know my ideaIts memory is on. Use a temporary chat, or turn memory off (privacy settings)
The Goldfish asked almost nothingAsk a follow-up in the same chat: "Which three things would you most likely get wrong if you built this now?"
The Goldfish asked fifteen questionsAnswer the ones about MUST stories first. Put the rest in Open questions, each with your best guess
My review partner isn't hereYour instructor joins you to another pair, or gives you the practice spec

Before you leave Studio B ​

  • ☐ SPEC.md and PROMPTS.md are committed in your new public repository, and Pages is switched on.
  • ☐ Your written spec review is on Canvas, or you know you'll finish it for homework.
  • ☐ Homework: homework. Your spec review and your reflection go on Canvas, written without AI.
